Find a structural feature in the real RNA

You learned these concepts with simple 3D models. Choose one here to locate a real example in the complete RNA structure.

Glycosidic angle (χ)

The glycosidic angle describes how a nucleobase is rotated around the bond that links it to ribose.

Example: A residue in the 1EHZ tRNA will be selected automatically and its four χ-defining atoms highlighted.

What should I notice? The four atoms define a dihedral angle. Changing χ swings the base relative to the sugar.

Choose “Show in 3D” to highlight this feature.

About the beta

A teaching model, not a folding simulation

The first three views introduce building blocks and simplified condensation schemes. Primary, secondary, and tertiary views describe the same tRNA; they do not imply a sequential folding pathway.

The default secondary view is a simplified educational map of a 76-nucleotide tRNA, and the default tertiary view uses the experimentally determined all-atom coordinates of yeast tRNA-Phe from PDB 1EHZ. Custom Secondary and 3D inputs can be linked only after residue-count/sequence validation and user confirmation that they represent the same RNA molecule.
